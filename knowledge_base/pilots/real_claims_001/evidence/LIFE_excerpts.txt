E-LIFE-DESIGN
The Lifestyle Interventions and Independence for Elders (LIFE) study was a multicenter, randomized trial that enrolled participants between February 2010 and December 2011, who participated for an average of 2.6 years. Follow-up ended in December 2013. Outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention assignment. Participants were recruited from urban, suburban and rural communities at 8 field centers throughout the US. We randomized a volunteer sample of 1,635 sedentary men and women aged 70–89 years who had physical limitations, defined as a score on the Short Physical Performance Battery of 9 or below, but were able to walk 400 m.

E-LIFE-RESULT
Incident major mobility disability occurred in 30.1% (n=246/818) of physical activity and 35.5% (n=290/817) of health education participants (HR=0.82, 95%CI=0.69–0.98, p=0.03).

E-LIFE-ENDPOINT
The primary outcome of major mobility disability was defined as the inability to complete a 400 m walk test within 15 min without sitting and without the help of another person or walker.

E-LIFE-ALTERNATIVE
Of the 246 and 290 physical activity and health education participants classified with major mobility disability, 42 (17%) and 32 (11%) resulted from alternative adjudications in each group, respectively.

E-LIFE-ANALYSIS
The effect of the intervention on the primary outcome (i.e. time until the initial ascertainment of major mobility disability) was tested based on a two-tailed significance of 0.05 using the intention to treat approach in which participants are grouped according to randomization assignment.

E-LIFE-DEATH
Given the small number of events the data regarding mortality are inconclusive.

E-LIFE-SAE
Serious adverse events were reported by 404/818 (49.4%) of the physical activity and 373/817 (45.7%) of the health education participants (Risk Ratio=1.08; 95%CI=0.98–1.20).
