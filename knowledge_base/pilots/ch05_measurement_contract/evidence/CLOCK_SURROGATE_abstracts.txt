# Yi M et al. Exp Gerontol 2026;226:113367, PMID 42926120, doi 10.1016/j.exger.2026.113367 (full text not read).
We systematically reviewed studies reporting pre-post changes in at least one DNA-methylation clock and one clinical outcome.
Twenty-six independent studies were included: 13 comparative and 13 single-arm pre-post studies.
Fifteen were mixed, six indeterminate, four jointly inconclusive, and one uncontrolled study uniformly concordant for intermediate outcomes; no comparative study or study of patient-important outcomes was uniformly concordant.
No intervention-clock-clinical outcome combination met the criteria for meta-analysis, and no trial-level surrogate-endpoint validation was identified. Certainty was very low across all assessed evidence bodies.
Epigenetic clocks should currently be considered exploratory or adjunctive biomarkers of intervention response rather than validated clinical surrogate endpoints.
# Borrus DS et al. Aging Cell 2026;25(10):e70756, PMID 42850743, PMC13650178.
In this study, we find that when extending previously published single-clock intervention studies to include additional clock models, no additional clocks reach statistical significance.
We then analyze clock behavior across positive control datasets (age-accelerating events) to propose three criteria that increase confidence in a result: (1) reliable PC-based clocks respond, (2) mortality-trained or pace-of-aging clocks respond, and (3) multiple clocks respond in the same direction.
